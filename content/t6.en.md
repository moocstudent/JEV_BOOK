## Reduce a headline number to its conditions

Jev's three headline numbers are 70-to-500 ms end to end, 96.6% on the v2 suite, and 0.870 on Banking77. This chapter teaches you to take such numbers apart rather than copy them into your design.

@fig t6-claim

- **70–500 ms** is a sevenfold spread whose width comes from state length, question count and the network; where you land is decided by your usage, not the model.
- **96.6%** is a macro average over a task set the vendor defined. Its value is comparing models on the same questions, not predicting yours.
- **0.870** is the most informative. High-cardinality intent classification over 77 labels is the hardest shape for this class of model, and the open encoder camp generally falls to around 0.4 on the same task, so that number says Jev really does have something on high cardinality.

## High cardinality is the stress test

As labels multiply, the token budget the options must squeeze into tightens (see LY2), the encoder camp falls away faster, and Jev barely moves. So to judge a System One model, do not look at how accurate it is on a four-way choice — look at whether it collapses on a seventy-seven-way one.

## The only thing that can promise your accuracy

A vendor benchmark can only **rank candidates**; it cannot promise your accuracy. The only thing that can is your own labelled data — which is what module VI teaches.
