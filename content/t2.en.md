## Three primitives, one softmax

System One collapses every decision into three primitives — not product simplification, but because those three shapes cover what programs actually need.

@fig t2-prims

- **choice** picks one item from a set fixed in advance and returns a distribution over the whole set. Mathematically a multiclass softmax, so the number of options sets the entropy of that distribution and the units of the confidence number.
- **score** rates on an ordered scale and returns the expectation of an ordinal distribution. It is a continuous number whose uncertainty lives in the shape rather than the number — a uniform and a bimodal distribution both give 1.5, and under the second neither mode is true.
- **noul** is the yes/no question returning the probability of yes. It is the only primitive whose **value is itself a probability**, and therefore the easiest to misread: 0.82 means about eight in ten such inputs are true, not that this one scores 82.

## k changes everything

The option count k is what this chapter nails down. At k=2 the random baseline is 0.5; at k=18 it is 0.056. Carrying one confidence threshold between those tasks is meaningless: 0.7 barely passes on a binary choice and is a strong signal on an eighteen-way one. The entropy ceiling log(k) also moves with k, so any entropy-based confidence cannot be compared across k directly.

**Exercise**: write your task as each of the three primitives once and see which fits. If you are using noul for a judgement that really has three outcomes (yes/no/unsure), what you want is a choice.
