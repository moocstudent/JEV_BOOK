## Three checkpoints and a router

Laya is not one model but three: `laya` (English, ModernBERT-large, 421M, 512 context), `laya-multilingual` (mmBERT-base, 322M, 1024 context, 100-plus languages, and faster because it is smaller), and `laya-typed-decisions` (fine-tuned on the training split of the typed-decisions benchmark).

@fig t10-router

A Router stands in front: sub-millisecond script and language detection decides which class the text belongs to, dispatches to the matching checkpoint, and returns its reasoning.

## Three routing cases

- **Non-Latin scripts** (Chinese, Arabic, Devanagari) go to the multilingual branch for an obvious reason.
- **Latin script that does not look like English** (German, Portuguese) also goes multilingual.
- **Very short Latin text** often carries no language signal and falls to default — which is English unless you change it. If most traffic is not English, set default to multilingual, or short inputs get silently misrouted.

## A hard constraint to nail down

On non-Latin scripts the English checkpoint does not degrade, it **collapses**: 0.000 accuracy on Khmer at a self-reported 95.2% confidence. The cleanest 'high confidence, total failure' in the book, and the real-world version of module III — high confidence is not correctness. Chinese must go multilingual, no exceptions.

**Exercise**: type text in several languages on the bench and watch routing decide and reason; then force Chinese to the English checkpoint and watch accuracy collapse while confidence lies to you.
