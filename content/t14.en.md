## The LLM camp: slower, holds up on many labels

The LLM camp's idea is to squeeze decisions out of a generative model you already have instead of training a new encoder.

@fig t14-llm

- **Kev** (Qwen3.5 + LoRA, 0.8B–9B, Apache-2.0): self-reported 0.837 for Kev-4B and 0.852 for Kev-9B, aligned to TypeSafe's System One API — the most direct candidate when you want Jev's accuracy but must self-host.
- **SemIf** (2B–4B, MIT): trains nothing, reads the logits of option tokens on a model you already run, 0.845 agreement, with MLX and WebGPU support. Its strategic value is **zero new components** — the model you already run is your decision engine.
- **NanoJev** (Qwen3 0.6B, MIT): for games and control loops.
- **OpenJev** (DiffusionGemma 26B-A4B, Apache-2.0): the largest, diffusion-style parallel decoding, needs vLLM and VRAM.
- **Decider** (Qwen3.5 2B): claims calibration without publishing a measurement — use it as an exercise in reading a claim.

## A selection rule

Past twenty labels, or wherever labels split finely by meaning, the encoder camp should yield to the LLM camp; and if you already run an LLM, try SemIf's approach first, because its marginal cost is close to zero.

**Exercise**: if an LLM is already in your stack, spend half an hour writing a SemIf-style logit-scoring function and compare its accuracy against the standalone decision model you were considering. Often you will find you need no extra service.
