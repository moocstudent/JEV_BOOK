## Four tables and a verdict template

An evaluation hands you a pile of numbers; this chapter fixes the order to read them in and when to stop.

@fig t18-report

- **Table 1 · accuracy vs three baselines**: did the model learn anything? Measured here, 0.505 against random 0.056 and majority 0.120 — yes.
- **Table 2 · median and P95 latency**: never the mean — a few cold starts drag it into meaninglessness. This book's first reading of 4467 ms was polluted by a concurrent download; the clean re-run gave 401 / 447 ms.
- **Table 3 · calibration**: ECE as-shipped and after fitting a temperature on held-out data. Measured here 0.215 → 0.141 (T=1.65), still above 0.1, so the probability cannot be used as a probability.
- **Table 4 · threshold-coverage-precision**: how much work does this remove?

## Order matters

If table 1 fails the baselines, the other three are irrelevant; if table 4 shows the threshold cannot buy precision, the first three cannot justify shipping. This book's run died at table 4: 0.5→0.95 gained only 6.6 points.

## The verdict template

Combinations of the four tables map onto three conclusions: **ship it** (with a threshold and fallback), **take another route** (fine-tune or an LLM), or **the data or taxonomy is the problem** (go back to chapter seventeen's agreement check).

**Exercise**: enter your own four sets of numbers on the bench and get the verdict with its reasoning. If it says 'another route', return to OS3 and see which alternative fits you.
