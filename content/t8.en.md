## One scalar, one fixed distribution

Temperature scaling is the highest-yield move in calibration: divide the logits by a scalar T and re-softmax. Above 1 flattens the distribution (curing overconfidence), below 1 sharpens it. It **changes no prediction's ranking** — the argmax is untouched, so accuracy does not move by a point — it only changes the probability values.

@fig t8-temp

That one change moves the mean ECE of Laya's English checkpoint from 0.466 to 0.081.

## How to fit it, and the mistake every tutorial makes

Minimise negative log-likelihood on **held-out** data. This book's implementation uses grid search over `[0.5, 5.0]` (matching Laya's runtime clamp): ten lines, no optimiser state, and it cannot fail to converge on a one-dimensional problem.

The mistake: fitting the temperature on the training set, or on the very data you plan to report, is self-deception. Fit and report on non-overlapping halves.

## Bucketing, and a real counter-example

One model is overconfident to different degrees at 2 options and at 18, so in practice a temperature is fitted per (question type × option count) bucket.

The counter-example: Laya's shipped `choice:11+` bucket is 0.1006, which does not cure overconfidence but **manufactures** it, inflating a 0.24 top probability to 0.99. The library added `TEMP_MIN = 0.5` to refuse it. If your task lands in the 11+ bucket (18 labels, say), the shipped calibration is already dead.

**Exercise**: fit a temperature on your held-out half, write it back to `agent.temperature`, and plot the reliability diagram again. You will see the points move toward the diagonal while accuracy does not budge.
